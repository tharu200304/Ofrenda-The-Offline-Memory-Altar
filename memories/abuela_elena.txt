# Elena Morales de Ramos (1932 – 2018)
## Notebook of Memories, Recipes, and Reflections

### 1. The Pan de Muerto Recipe (Oaxaca, 1974)
The pan de muerto cannot be rushed. If you hurry it, the bread will turn dense and lose its spirit.
- 500 grams unbleached wheat flour
- 150 grams raw cane sugar
- 100 grams unsalted butter, softened at room temperature
- 3 whole farm eggs plus 2 egg yolks
- 2 tablespoons of distilled orange blossom water (agua de azahar)
- The finely grated zest of two large ripe oranges
- 15 grams active dry yeast dissolved in 1/4 cup warm whole milk
- 1 pinch of sea salt
- Melted butter and coarse sugar for dusting after baking

The secret is the orange blossom water and kneading until your forearms ache. When we lived in Oaxaca in the summer of 1974, your grandfather Mateo would sit on the shaded courtyard patio playing boleros on his Spanish cedar guitar while the dough rose under a damp linen cloth in the warm midday sun. The whole courtyard smelled of citrus zest and yeast. Always shape the crossbones on top with care; they represent the four cardinal directions and the tears shed for those who crossed the veil.

### 2. Meeting Mateo in the Alameda Central (Mexico City, October 1951)
I met Mateo beneath the jacaranda trees near the fountain of Alameda Central. He was wearing his blue Sunday linen jacket and holding a small wooden box he had carved by hand from fragrant cedar wood. When he handed it to me, he blushed and said: "A box for your secrets, señorita." Inside was a tiny dried marigold flower. We walked along Juárez Avenue until dusk, eating roasted sweet potatoes sold from a cart with hot steam whistling into the crisp evening air. We listened to an organillero play "Solamente Una Vez" by Agustín Lara. Sixty-four years we walked together after that afternoon.

### 3. The Path of Marigolds (Cempasúchil)
Every year on the 31st of October, when the twilight settles over the mountains, you must pluck the petals of fresh cempasúchil flowers. Do not crush them—scatter them gently in an unbroken golden line starting from the front street gate all the way to the threshold of the altar room. The ancient ones taught us that the scent of the flower and its bright yellow color are the lantern that lights the way through the mist of the underworld. If the line is broken, the souls wander in uncertainty. Place a glass of cool well water next to the candle; traveling between worlds leaves the spirit with an unbearable thirst.

### 4. What Remains
When people ask me if I am afraid of growing old, I tell them: "Death is only an absence of breath, never an absence of love." Everything you give with an open heart remains woven into the wood of the house, into the taste of the bread, and into the stories your children whisper when the nights grow long. If you ever feel lost, light a single beeswax candle, breathe in the scent of oranges and cedar, and I will be there beside you.
