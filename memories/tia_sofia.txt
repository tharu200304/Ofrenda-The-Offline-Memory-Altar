# Sofía Ramos Morales (1955 – 2022)
## Field Notes, Botanical Remedies, and Highland Postcards

### 1. The Monarch Butterfly Sanctuary (Sierra Chincua, Michoacán - November 1993)
The sanctuary at Sierra Chincua in early November is holy ground. Millions of monarch butterflies (Danaus plexippus) cling to the oyamel fir branches in golden-orange clusters so thick the boughs sag under their collective weight. When the midday sun strikes through the high canopy, the entire forest begins to flutter and hum with the sound of millions of velvety wings. 
The Purépecha elders taught us that the butterflies arrive each year on the first day of November because they carry the spirits of the children who returned home for the feast. When a butterfly lands upon your shoulder, you do not swat it away; you hold your breath and say a quiet thank you.

### 2. Traditional Herbal Remedies
- For grief and heavy-hearted sleeplessness: Boil dried blossoms of Mexican orange blossom (flor de azahar) with two heads of German chamomile and a teaspoon of raw wildflower honey. Sip slowly in a darkened room before sleep.
- For digestive chills and stomach heaviness: Steep wild peppermint leaves (hierbabuena) with a thin slice of fresh ginger and a crushed star anise.
- Marigold Infusion (Té de Cempasúchil): Harvest organic orange cempasúchil petals in the early morning before the dew evaporates. Dry them flat on unbleached brown paper in a dry, ventilated pantry away from direct sunlight. A pinch steeped in hot water relieves autumn chills and calms nervous tension.

### 3. The Turquoise and Silver Earrings
In my cedar travel trunk in the bedroom closet, inside a violet velvet pouch, you will find my favorite silver hoop earrings with rough-cut turquoise stones from Taxco. My father Mateo bought them for me on my eighteenth birthday after selling a flamenco guitar to a traveler from Seville. I leave them to my eldest niece. Wear them whenever you need courage to speak truth to people who would rather hear polite lies.
